# Query proposals — 2026-09-15 (second version)

**Proposed; the first 16 new pairs and the idea behind them are approved by the author.** This version replaces the first one. It keeps those 16 pairs, adds the author's own gift queries and his "things the shop does not sell", adds queries shaped like real shopper queries, and classifies everything with Baymard's eight query types instead of a list made up here.

## The purpose, in one paragraph

The golden set is the exam a search engine sits. Almost every question on it today names the product ("mochila", "cafetera 12 tazas"), which is exactly what word matching already answers — so hybrid search was being examined on lexical search's home ground. The queries below are the ones a shopper types when they do not know what the product is called, want something *without* a feature, write a measure in shorthand, or ask for something the shop does not have. Every query is paired with its counterpart in the other language, and each pair is put in one of two piles before anything is tuned: **tune** (used to adjust hybrid search) and **holdout** (kept aside and only used to check that an adjustment was not a fit to the tune pile).

## Where the query types come from

- **Baymard Institute, *Ecommerce search query types*** — eight types, and how many sites fail each: exact 12 %, product type 20 %, feature 39 %, use case 43 %, compatibility 44 %, abbreviation & symbol 54 %, symptom 37 %, non-product 66 %. https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-query-types
- **Amazon Science, *Shopping Queries Dataset* (ESCI, Apache-2.0)** — 15,180 real Spanish and 97,345 real English shopper queries, read on 2026-09-15 for how people actually phrase the categories Tendero sells. No ESCI query is copied verbatim; where a proposal is shaped after one, the row says so. https://github.com/amazon-science/esci-data

What the ESCI sample showed, and why it shaped the list:

| observed in real queries | es | en | what it means for the exam |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| gift / recipient ("regalo para niña de 13 años") | 177 | 6,209 | common: use-case queries belong in the exam |
| measure or abbreviation ("olla a presion 12 litros") | 332 | 1,816 | common: a type we had none of |
| negation with *sin* / *without* ("lámpara sin cables") | frequent in the sample | frequent in the sample | a feature query word matching gets backwards |
| a full question ("que regalar a tu pareja") | 8 | 282 | rare in Spanish: one question is enough |
| symptom ("non slip", "odor") | 12 | 85 | rare but real |

The counts come from simple patterns over unique queries and are an order of magnitude, not a census.

## Kinds used in the golden set

Baymard's names, so a report reads against a published reference: `exact`, `product-type`, `feature`, `use-case`, `abbreviation`, `compatibility`, `symptom`, `non-product` — plus `not-sold`, which Baymard does not name and a shop meets every day. A typo or a synonym is noted in the table but is not a type of its own: Baymard files both under the type the query would have had spelled right.

## New: queries with no product to answer them

Tendero sells none of these, or they are not about a product. They are **not** scored with NDCG — there is nothing relevant to rank. They measure the opposite failure: a search that answers anyway. Lexical search with an OR fallback can match "casa" in house slippers; the vector side always returns its nearest neighbours, however far. The metric is how many results a strategy shows, and whether any looks like an answer; it is designed with the code, before these are scored.

## The split, decided before measuring

Within each kind, pairs are taken in the order of this file — E, N, A, R, then X — and alternate tune, holdout, tune… A pair lands in one split whole. Result: **28 scored pairs to tune and 24 held out, plus 8 no-answer pairs, 4 on each side.** The one typo pair falls in holdout this time, so tuning never sees a typo — a known gap, written down rather than discovered.

## Counts

Each culture ends with **52 scored queries** — 12 product-type, 16 feature, 12 use-case, 3 symptom, 3 abbreviation, 3 compatibility, 3 exact in Spanish; English has 13 product-type and 15 feature, because E12's English half ("trail running shirt") names a product type where the Spanish one ("camiseta hombre") names a feature — and **8 no-answer queries**. Counted from the committed files after tagging: 28 scored pairs in tune, 24 in holdout, and 4 no-answer pairs on each side. The new ones are judged against the whole catalogue of 100 products, so they start with no pooling bias; the author reviews the criteria, as with the pool.
